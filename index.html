Research Projects

3D printing of photoactive acrylate (nano-composite) resins

Lead Researcher

High filler loadings in nanocomposite resins pose significant challenges to DLP 3D printing of functional materials. I formulated a model silica–resin system to systematically investigate the relationship between resin rheology, printability, and printed-part properties. This study identified key processing challenges and revealed the limitations of shear rheology in predicting DLP printability.

Photo-rheologyStructure-property RelationshipsThermoset Resins

Flow-induced crystallization of polylactic acid (PLA)

Lead Researcher

High crystallinity in PLA limits its enzymatic degradation. I employed flow-induced crystallization (FIC) using a shear rheometer to tailor the crystalline morphology. This approach enhanced enzymatic degradation by 8 percentage points.

Melt RheologyFlow-induced CrystallizationThermoplastics

Development of organoselenium-based antimicrobial textiles

Lead Researcher

Developing antimicrobial textiles without disrupting conventional textile manufacturing remains a challenge. We chemically functionalized cotton fabrics with reactive organoselenium compounds as novel antimicrobial agents. The modified fabrics achieved an 8-log reduction in microbial populations of S. aureus and C. albicans.

FunctionalizationAntimicrobial AssayCollaboration

Cure kinetics and network formation in PDMS bottlebrush elastomers

Contributing Researcher

Bottlebrush elastomers have grafted side chains that suppress entanglements, so their network formation differs from linear polymers. Using time-resolved rheology during catalytic curing, we tracked the sol–gel transition and showed that side chains lower the fractal dimension of the critical gel cluster. Time-cure superposition revealed a universal network formation process.

Polymer FormulationTime-cure superpositionElastomers

Filler particle transport within rubber matrix under tire–road contact

Contributing Researcher

Filler particles released from tires are an environmental concern, so it is important to understand how fillers move inside the rubber under the loading a tire experiences while driving. We studied particle motion in the rubber matrix using X-ray photon correlation spectroscopy (XPCS).
